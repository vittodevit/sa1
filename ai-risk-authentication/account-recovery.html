Account Recovery and Password Resets

Everyone forgets a password sometimes, so websites offer a "Forgot password?" option. If an attacker can misuse it they may access your account without knowing your password. That makes account recovery a possible back door into your account.

How a password reset works

Here's what usually happens when you reset a password:

  1. Step 1

    You click "Forgot password?"

  2. Step 2

    You enter your email address or phone number.

  3. Step 3

    The website sends you a reset link or code.

  4. Step 4

    You use it to choose a new password and regain access.

Whoever controls the email account or phone number used for recovery may be able to reset your password.

Why recovery is a weak point

Password recovery can create another way for attackers to take over an account.

  • Security questions: Answers about your birthday or where you were born may be easy to guess or find online.
  • SMS codes and SIM swapping: An attacker may trick your phone company into moving your number to their SIM card, so they receive your reset codes.
  • The recovery email: If someone gets into your email account they may use it to reset passwords for your other accounts.

Real example: Sarah Palin (2008)

During the 2008 election campaign a student reset Sarah Palin's Yahoo email password using the "Forgot password?" process instead of guessing her password. He answered security questions using information he found online including her birthday, ZIP code and where she met her husband.

How websites make recovery safer

Websites can use these practices to make password resets harder to misuse.

Safer password reset practices
Good practice Why it helps
Reset links expire quickly and work only once. Old or stolen links become useless.
Show the same message whether or not an account exists. Attackers can't check which email addresses have accounts.
Send an email after every password change. You can quickly spot a change you didn't make.
Log out other sessions after a reset. This can remove an attacker who was already signed in.
Don't use security questions. The answers may be easy to find or guess.
