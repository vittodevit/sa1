AI Risks

Personal Data Profiling and Inference

While traditional data collection gathers what we explicitly share (like filling out a form), Artificial Intelligence takes privacy risks to a new level through profiling and inference. AI systems can combine seemingly harmless pieces of information to deduce highly sensitive details about our lives, such as our health conditions, political views, or sexual orientation.

How AI Connects the Dots

Data profiling is the process of examining, analyzing, and creating useful summaries of data. AI algorithms do this automatically at a massive scale using a technique called data fusion. They link isolated data points from different sources to build a detailed digital twin of a person.

For example, an AI might notice that people who buy unscented lotion and cotton balls often have a baby. If it combines this with your location data (visiting a maternity clinic) and your search history (looking at cribs), it can confidently infer that you are pregnant—even if you never typed "I am pregnant" online.

The Power of Inference

Inference is the act of deriving a conclusion based on evidence and reasoning. AI doesn't just collect what you explicitly tell it; it infers what you don't say. Here are some real-world examples:

"Individually, a single piece of data is harmless. Combined, it becomes a weapon against privacy."

  • Health: Searching for symptoms at 3 AM and buying specific over-the-counter medication can lead an AI to predict a chronic health condition.
  • Financial Status: The type of phone you use, your battery percentage, and your typing speed can be used to infer your income level or creditworthiness.
  • Mental Health: The speed at which you scroll, the time of day you are active, and the tone of your posts can be analyzed to detect signs of depression or anxiety.

The Real-World Risks

This inferred data is highly valuable. It is often sold to data brokers, insurance companies, and advertisers. The main risks include:

Price Discrimination

AI might show you higher prices for flights or products because it infers you are wealthy or in a desperate situation.

Algorithmic Exclusion

You might be denied a loan, a job interview, or health insurance based on an inferred characteristic that you have no way to prove or correct.

Manipulation

Micro-targeting allows advertisers or political campaigns to manipulate your behavior based on your specific psychological profile.

How to Protect Yourself

While you cannot completely disappear from the internet, you can reduce your data footprint:

  • Limit app permissions: Deny access to your location, microphone, and contacts for apps that don't strictly need them.
  • Use privacy-focused tools: Use browsers that block third-party cookies (like Brave) and search engines that don't track you (like DuckDuckGo).
  • Practice data minimization: Only share what is absolutely necessary. Never post your exact location or personal milestones in real-time.
