AI Risks

AI-Generated Disinformation

The rapid advancement of Artificial Intelligence has made it easier than ever to create and spread false information. AI can now generate highly convincing text, images, audio, and video at a scale that threatens to overwhelm our ability to distinguish fact from fiction.

How AI is Changing the Threat Landscape

Generative AI tools have lowered the barrier to entry for malicious actors. Creating a fake news article or a deepfake video no longer requires a team of experts; it can be done in seconds by anyone with an internet connection.

Fake Text (LLMs)

Large Language Models can write thousands of convincing fake articles, social media posts, and reviews in seconds, flooding the internet with propaganda.

Deepfakes (Video/Audio)

AI can clone a person's face and voice. Scammers use this to impersonate CEOs, politicians, or family members to demand money or spread lies.

Bot Networks

AI-powered bots can create fake accounts, post automatically, and interact with each other to make a false narrative appear trending and popular.

Real-World Examples

AI disinformation is not a future problem; it is already happening and influencing the real world. Here are some notable cases:

  • March 2023

    The Pope in a Puffer Jacket

    An AI-generated image of Pope Francis wearing a stylish white puffer jacket went viral. Many people believed it was real, showing how easily AI can blur the lines between reality and fiction.

  • 2024 Elections

    Robocalls Impersonating Politicians

    During the US primaries, an AI-generated audio clip mimicked President Joe Biden's voice, telling voters to stay home. This highlighted the severe danger of AI in manipulating democratic processes.

  • Ongoing

    Financial Scams via Voice Cloning

    Scammers use short clips of a person's voice from social media to clone it, calling their relatives to urgently demand money for a fake emergency.

The "Liar's Dividend"

This is one of the most dangerous consequences of AI. Because deepfakes exist, people can now dismiss real evidence as "fake news" or AI-generated. For example, a corrupt politician caught on video saying something incriminating might claim it's a deepfake, making it harder to hold people accountable for their actions.

How to Protect Yourself from Disinformation

As AI gets better at lying, human critical thinking becomes our best defense. Follow these steps:

  • Lateral Reading: Don't just trust the website you are on. Open new tabs and search for the same claim on reputable fact-checking sites (like Snopes or Reuters Fact Check).
  • Check the Source: Is the account real? Does it have a history of posting reliable content? Be skeptical of emotional or sensational headlines.
  • Reverse Image Search: If a photo looks too shocking to be true, use Google Images or TinEye to see if it has been taken out of context from an older event.
  • Pause Before Sharing: Disinformation relies on outrage and speed. If something makes you incredibly angry, wait 24 hours before sharing it.
