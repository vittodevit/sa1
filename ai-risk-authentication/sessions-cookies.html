Sessions, Cookies and Staying Logged in

After you log in, a website needs to remember you as you move between pages. It uses a session and a cookie to do this. If someone steals your cookie, they may be able to access your account as if they were you.

How websites remember you

After you log in, the website creates a session with a random session ID. Your browser saves the ID in a cookie and sends it back to the website as you browse.

  1. Step 1

    You log in.

  2. Step 2

    The website creates a session and session ID.

  3. Step 3

    Your browser saves the ID in a cookie.

  4. Step 4

    Your browser sends the cookie, and the website recognizes you.

Staying logged in

A normal session may end when you close your browser or after some time. Choosing “Remember me” can keep you logged in for days or weeks, which is convenient on your own phone. On a shared computer, it’s risky because the next person may still be able to use your account.

Session hijacking

A session cookie is like a hotel key card, whoever has it may be able to get in. If an attacker steals your cookie, they can use your session as if they were you, without entering your password or a new two-factor authentication code.
