CYBERSECURITY / FAMOUS INCIDENTS

Introduction and the Earliest Famous Incidents

Introduction

Computers and the internet have become an important part of everyday life. People use them to communicate, study, work and share information. However, as technology has developed, new security problems have also appeared. Cybersecurity incidents can affect individuals, companies and governments. This section explores the history of these incidents, important attacks and the ways cybersecurity has changed over time.

What Are Cybersecurity Incidents?

A cybersecurity incident is an event that affects the security of a computer, network or digital information. These incidents can happen in different ways. For example, attackers may access systems without permission, steal personal information, damage files or stop services from working. Some incidents happen because of software weaknesses, while others are caused by human mistakes.

The Beginning of Cybersecurity

In the early days of computing, computers were mostly separate machines. Later, networks such as ARPANET allowed computers in different places to communicate with each other. This was an important step in the development of the modern internet. However, connecting computers also created new security risks. Programs could move between computers, and problems in one system could affect other connected systems.

Creeper (1971)

Creeper was an experimental computer program created by Bob Thomas in 1971. It was designed to move between computers connected to ARPANET, an early computer network. When it reached a computer, it displayed the message "I'm the creeper, catch me if you can!" on the screen.

Creeper was not created to steal information or damage computers. It was an experiment that showed how a program could move between connected machines. It became an important example in the early history of computer worms and network security.

The Morris Worm (1988)

The Morris Worm appeared on November 2, 1988. It was created by Robert Tappan Morris, a computer science student at Cornell University. The program spread through the internet and affected computers running certain versions of the UNIX operating system.

The worm used weaknesses in computer systems and spread from one machine to another. It copied itself many times, which caused computers to become very slow or stop working. Within 24 hours, around 6,000 of the 60,000 computers connected to the internet at that time were affected.

The Morris Worm was important because it showed how quickly a computer program could spread through a network and cause serious problems. It also increased awareness of computer security and the need to respond to cyberattacks.

Why Were These Incidents Important?

Creeper and the Morris Worm were important events in the early history of cybersecurity. Creeper showed that programs could move between connected computers, while the Morris Worm showed how a program could cause widespread disruption. These events helped people understand the risks of connected systems and the importance of protecting computers and networks.
