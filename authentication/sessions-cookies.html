Sessions, Cookies and Staying Logged in

After you log in, a website needs to remember you as you move between pages. It uses a session and a cookie to do this. If someone steals your cookie, they may be able to access your account as if they were you.

How websites remember you

After you log in, the website creates a session with a random session ID. Your browser saves the ID in a cookie and sends it back to the website as you browse.

  1. Step 1

    You log in.

  2. Step 2

    The website creates a session and session ID.

  3. Step 3

    Your browser saves the ID in a cookie.

  4. Step 4

    Your browser sends the cookie, and the website recognizes you.

Staying logged in

A normal session may end when you close your browser or after some time. Choosing “Remember me” can keep you logged in for days or weeks, which is convenient on your own phone. On a shared computer, it’s risky because the next person may still be able to use your account.

Session hijacking

A session cookie is like a hotel key card, whoever has it may be able to get in. If an attacker steals your cookie, they can use your session as if they were you, without entering your password or a new two-factor authentication code.

Attackers may steal session cookies in several ways:

  • Malware: A harmful program on your computer can copy cookies from your browser.
  • Unsafe Wi-Fi: On websites without HTTPS, someone on the same network may be able to read cookies sent over the connection. Learn more about Public Wi-Fi risks.
  • Bad scripts (XSS): Harmful code on a website may read cookies that are not protected from scripts and send them to an attacker.

Real example: Linus Tech Tips (2023)

In March 2023, an employee opened malware disguised as a sponsorship offer and attackers took over the Linus Tech Tips YouTube channel. The malware stole session cookies, letting the hackers access the account without entering the password or a new 2FA code and stream cryptocurrency scam videos.

How websites protect sessions

Websites can use cookie settings to help protect session information.

Cookie settings that protect sessions
Setting What it does
Secure Sends the cookie only over HTTPS.
HttpOnly Stops page scripts from directly reading the cookie.
SameSite Controls whether the cookie is sent with requests from other websites.

Websites can also use these protections to keep sessions safer:

  • Session expiration: Ends the session after a period of inactivity.
  • New session ID after login: Replaces the old ID so it can’t be reused for the new session.
  • Real logout: Ends the session on the website’s side as well as clearing the cookie in your browser.
  • Asking for the password again: Requires you to confirm your password before important changes such as changing your email.

Keep your sessions safe

  • Log out when you use a shared computer.
  • Don’t select “Remember me” on public devices.
  • Check active sessions or devices in your account settings, and log out of any you don’t recognize.
