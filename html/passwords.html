Passwords and Passphrases

Passwords are the most common method of authentication, acting as the first line of defense for our digital accounts. However, poor password habits remain one of the biggest security risks today.

Length and Complexity

When creating a password, length is often more important than complexity. While adding symbols and numbers helps, a longer password takes significantly more time for a computer to crack using brute-force attacks.

  • Length: Aim for at least 12 to 16 characters.
  • Complexity: Use a mix of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols.

The Danger of Password Reuse

Using the same password across multiple accounts is extremely dangerous. If one website suffers a data breach, attackers will use that stolen password to try and log into your other accounts (a technique known as credential stuffing). Every account should have a unique password.

Creating Strong, Memorable Passphrases

A passphrase is a sequence of words that is easy for a human to remember but very hard for a computer to guess. Instead of a short, confusing password like P@ssw0rd!, a passphrase like BlueHorseEatingAnApple! is much longer and more secure.

Tips for strong passphrases:

  1. Pick 3 to 4 random, unrelated words.
  2. Add a number or a symbol for extra security.
  3. Do not use famous quotes or lyrics, as these can be easily guessed.
