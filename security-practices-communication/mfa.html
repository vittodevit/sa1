Multi-factor authentication

Even if someone steals your password, a second factor stops them. A 2023 Microsoft study found that MFA lowered the risk of an account being hacked by 99.22%.

MFA combines multiple independent categories of verification to secure accounts.

Three kinds of factors

Something you know Something you have Something you are
Password, PIN Phone, security key Fingerprint, face

From weakest to strongest

  1. SMS code: better than nothing, but can be stolen by SIM swapping.
  2. Authenticator app code: generates secure codes directly on your device.
  3. Push notification with number matching: interactive approval on your phone.
  4. Passkey or security key: phishing-resistant because nothing is typed into fake sites.

Real example: Uber, 2022

An attacker with a stolen password sent push notification after push notification until an employee accepted one ("MFA fatigue"), allowing entry into internal systems. Never approve a login you didn't start.

Quick tips

  • Turn on MFA for your email today.
  • Prefer an app or passkey over SMS.
  • Never approve a login you didn't start.

Source: Microsoft Research (2023); FIDO Alliance; Appsecco
