Keeping online meetings under control

A meeting host decides who enters and who can present. Set those controls before a group call starts, rather than trying to find them during a disruption.

Controlling who joins your online meeting.

Before people join

  • Send the invitation only to the intended group.
  • Enable a waiting room or lobby where available, and decide who will admit participants.
  • Start with screen sharing limited to the host; allow a presenter when needed.

During the meeting

  • Review people waiting to join. Check an unfamiliar participant with the organiser before admitting them.
  • If everyone expected has arrived, consider locking the meeting. Late arrivals will need you to unlock it.
  • Know where the remove-participant and stop-sharing controls are.

Example: a group presentation

One student hosts while another presents. The host watches the waiting room and grants sharing access to the presenter. This leaves someone available to manage the call while the slides are being explained.

Does encryption stop an invited person disrupting a call?

No. Connection protection and participant permissions address different problems. See encryption basics for the separate explanation of how information is protected.

Available controls depend on the service, account and administrator settings. The sources below describe Zoom as one example.
