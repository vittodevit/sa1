Password hygiene

Length beats complexity, and every account needs its own password. If one website gets hacked and you used the same password elsewhere, attackers will try it everywhere—a tactic known as credential stuffing.

Using unique passphrases and a password manager protects your accounts against automated stuffing attacks.

What makes a good password

  • At least 15 characters if it is your only login step (NIST guidelines, 2025).
  • Passphrases: three or more random words (e.g., coffee-tractor-moon-lamp). Long and easy to remember.
  • Unique per account: especially for your email account, since email can be used to reset all your other services.
  • No forced 90-day changes: only change passwords when there is evidence of a leak.

Password managers

A password manager creates and saves a unique password for every site. You only remember one master password. You can check if your email appeared in a data leak at haveibeenpwned.com. Passkeys are an even safer option, see Authentication.

Quick tips

  • Use a passphrase of at least 15 characters.
  • Never reuse your email password.
  • Let a password manager do the remembering.

Source: NIST SP 800-63B Rev. 4; UK NCSC Cyber Aware; Verizon DBIR 2025
