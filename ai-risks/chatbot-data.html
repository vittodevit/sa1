Sensitive Data Shared with AI Chatbots

Chatbots such as ChatGPT or Gemini have become part of how we study and work, yet everything we type is transmitted to the provider's servers. Once personal or confidential information has been submitted, we lose control over it.

What happens to what you type?

Stored on servers

Conversations are retained by the provider, often for long periods. A software bug or a data breach is enough to expose them.

Used for training

Many services use conversations to train future models. This can usually be disabled in the settings, although few users do so. Information absorbed during training is extremely difficult to remove afterwards.

Read by people

A share of conversations is reviewed by staff for quality control and abuse detection. A conversation with a chatbot is therefore less private than it appears.

What you should never share

Data that should never go into a chatbot
Data Example Risk
Passwords and codes Your e-mail password, a 2FA code High
ID and bank details Passport number, IBAN High
Health information A medical report with your name on it High
Other people's data A friend's address, a classmate's grades Medium
Unpublished work or code Code from your company Medium

Real cases

  1. March 2023

    A bug shows other users' chats

    A bug in an open-source library used by ChatGPT allowed some users to see the titles of other people's conversations. Payment details of a small number of subscribers were exposed as well.

  2. 2023

    Samsung engineers leak source code

    Samsung engineers pasted confidential source code into ChatGPT while looking for help with it. The company responded by restricting the use of generative AI internally.

  3. 2023 and 2024

    Italy blocks ChatGPT

    In 2023 the Italian data protection authority suspended ChatGPT for roughly a month. In December 2024 it fined OpenAI 15 million euros for privacy violations.

  4. 2025

    Shared chats appear on Google

    Conversations that users had shared through a public link began to appear in Google search results. OpenAI withdrew the feature shortly afterwards.

How to protect yourself

  • Think before you paste: anything you would not publish online does not belong in a chatbot.
  • Remove names: replace real names and figures with placeholders before typing a text, not only before sending it (some websites record what is typed in a field even before it is submitted).
  • Check the settings: disable training on your conversations and use temporary chats where available.
  • Follow the rules: in a professional context, use only the tools approved by your organization.
