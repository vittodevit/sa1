Ransomware and Destructive Attacks

Ransomware is a type of malicious software that blocks access to computer systems or encrypts files. Attackers usually demand money, called a ransom, in exchange for restoring access. Destructive attacks, on the other hand, are designed to damage or destroy data and systems.

1. WannaCry Ransomware Attack (May 2017)

Background

WannaCry was a major ransomware attack that began on 12 May 2017. It affected computers in more than 150 countries and became one of the most widespread cyberattacks in history. Its main target was computers running Microsoft Windows.

How did it happen?

WannaCry exploited a Windows security vulnerability known as EternalBlue. This weakness allowed the malware to spread between vulnerable computers through networks. Once inside a computer, it encrypted files and displayed a message demanding payment in Bitcoin to restore access. Microsoft had released a security update, but many organizations had not installed it.

Impact

More than 200,000 computers were affected in over 150 countries. The UK's National Health Service (NHS) experienced serious disruption. Hospitals cancelled appointments and some medical services were interrupted. Businesses and organizations also suffered operational and financial losses.

Importance

WannaCry demonstrated how quickly ransomware can spread across international networks. It also showed that failing to install security updates can put essential services, especially healthcare systems, at serious risk.

2. NotPetya Destructive Attack (June 2017)

Background

NotPetya was a destructive cyberattack that began in Ukraine in June 2017. Although it initially appeared to be ransomware, its main purpose was considered to be causing destruction rather than making money. It quickly spread beyond Ukraine and affected international organizations.

How did it happen?

The attack initially entered computers through a compromised software update for M.E.Doc, a Ukrainian accounting program. After infecting one computer, NotPetya spread through connected networks by exploiting security weaknesses and stealing login credentials. Unlike ordinary ransomware, it was designed to make data recovery extremely difficult or impossible.

Impact

Organizations in several countries were affected. Shipping company Maersk experienced major disruption to its global operations. Pharmaceutical company Merck also suffered serious operational damage. The worldwide economic damage was estimated at billions of dollars.

Importance

NotPetya demonstrated that trusted software updates can become a way for attackers to enter computer systems. It also showed that destructive malware can spread internationally and cause significant damage to companies, supply chains and the global economy.

3. International Impact

Both WannaCry and NotPetya demonstrated how cyberattacks can affect countries and organizations far beyond their original targets.

  • Global connectivity: Connected computer networks allowed malware to spread across national borders within a short period.
  • Supply chains: Disruptions to international companies affected their partners, customers and business operations.
  • Economic consequences: Organizations faced recovery costs, lost productivity and financial damage.
  • Cybersecurity awareness: These incidents encouraged governments and businesses to reconsider how they protect digital infrastructure.

4. Differences Between WannaCry and NotPetya

Feature WannaCry NotPetya
Year May 2017 June 2017
Initial spread Windows vulnerability (EternalBlue) Compromised M.E.Doc update
Main objective Demand ransom Cause destruction
Effect on files Encrypted files Damaged or destroyed data
Recovery Potentially possible Extremely difficult
Main characteristic Ransomware Destructive malware

The main difference is that WannaCry aimed to make money by encrypting files and demanding payment. NotPetya displayed a ransom message but was primarily designed to cause irreversible damage.

WannaCry and NotPetya were two major cyberattacks in 2017, but their objectives were different. WannaCry focused on encrypting files and demanding ransom, while NotPetya was designed primarily to destroy data and disrupt organizations. Both demonstrated how quickly malware can spread internationally and why software security, regular updates and reliable backup systems are essential.
