Who Detects and Responds to Cybersecurity Incidents?

1. Who Is Responsible for Detecting and Responding to Incidents?

Cybersecurity incidents are usually handled by specialized professionals within an organization. Their responsibility is to identify suspicious activities, investigate what happened and coordinate the response.

  • Cybersecurity Analysts: They monitor computer systems, examine security alerts and identify unusual activities that may indicate an attack.
  • Incident Response Teams: These teams coordinate the actions taken after an incident is detected. They investigate the situation, contain the threat and help restore normal operations.
  • Digital Forensic Investigators: They examine computers, networks and digital evidence to understand how an attack happened, what information was accessed and who may be responsible.
  • IT Specialists: They help identify affected systems, restore services and provide technical support during the response.

These professionals often work together because responding to a cybersecurity incident requires both technical investigation and coordinated decision-making.

2. The Role of Cybersecurity Analysts

Cybersecurity analysts are often among the first professionals to identify a possible incident.

  • Monitoring security alerts: They examine notifications generated by security systems to identify suspicious activities.
  • Identifying threats: They investigate unusual logins, unexpected network activity or unauthorized access.
  • Analyzing information: They examine system logs and other technical information to determine whether an actual security incident has occurred.
  • Reporting incidents: When a threat is confirmed, they inform the incident response team and provide the relevant details.

Their analysis helps organizations understand whether an event is harmless or requires an immediate response.

3. Incident Response Teams and Their Responsibilities

Incident response teams are responsible for managing the situation after a cybersecurity incident has been confirmed.

  • Coordinating the response: Organizing the people and resources involved in handling the incident.
  • Assessing the situation: Determining which systems are affected and how serious the incident is.
  • Containing the incident: Taking action to limit the attack and prevent it from spreading further.
  • Supporting recovery: Helping restore affected systems and return operations to normal.
  • Documenting actions: Recording the decisions and activities performed throughout the response.

The main goal of an incident response team is to manage the incident, reduce its impact and restore normal operations as safely as possible.

4. Stages of Handling a Cybersecurity Incident

After an incident is detected and confirmed, organizations follow several stages to investigate and manage the situation.

1. Detection and Identification

The organization examines the security alert to confirm whether a real incident has occurred. The team identifies the type of incident, when it started and which systems may be affected.

2. Investigation and Analysis

Cybersecurity professionals collect and examine digital evidence, including system logs, network activity and affected files. They try to determine how the attack happened, which systems were accessed and whether sensitive information was stolen or damaged.

3. Containment

Once the incident is understood, the response team takes action to limit its impact. This may involve disconnecting affected devices from the network, disabling compromised accounts or isolating affected systems to stop the incident from spreading.

4. Recovery

After the threat has been contained, organizations work to restore affected systems and services. This may include recovering data from backups, repairing damaged systems and checking that everything is functioning correctly before returning to normal operations.

5. Follow-up and Documentation

After the incident has been handled, the organization documents what happened, the damage caused and the actions taken. The team reviews the response and identifies what can be improved when handling similar incidents in the future.

5. How Organizations Determine What Happened and Assess the Damage

Organizations use digital evidence and technical investigations to understand the full impact of a cybersecurity incident.

  • System logs: Records of computer and network activities help investigators identify unusual actions and establish when the incident occurred.
  • Digital evidence: Files, emails and other electronic information can help reconstruct the events and understand how the attacker gained access.
  • Affected systems: Investigators identify which computers, servers, databases and networks were compromised.
  • Data assessment: Organizations determine whether personal information, financial records or confidential business data were accessed, stolen, changed or deleted.
  • Damage assessment: The organization evaluates the operational, financial and technical consequences of the incident.

By combining this information, investigators can reconstruct the attack, understand its consequences and prepare a detailed incident report.
