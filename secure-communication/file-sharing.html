Safe file sharing

A document link is an access decision. Check who can open the file, what they can do with it, and when that access should end.

Choose who can access your files before sharing them.

Before you send the link

  1. Choose the audience. For a group assignment, invite the named accounts that need the document. A link that works for anyone who receives it can reach people outside your group.
  2. Choose what they can do. Give editing access to co-authors. Someone checking the finished work may only need viewing or commenting access.
  3. Check the folder as well. A document can inherit access from its parent folder. Use a separate restricted folder when the audience should be smaller.
  4. Review access after the task. Remove invitations and broad links that are no longer needed. Keep the final submission available to the people who must assess it.

Example: sending a group report

You want two classmates to revise a report and a tutor to read it. Invite the classmates as editors and the tutor as a viewer. Before sending the link, check the access list rather than assuming that copying a link makes it private.

Removing access cannot recall every copy

A recipient may already have downloaded the file or captured its contents. Restrictions on downloading and copying cannot prevent every form of redistribution. Share only the information the recipient actually needs.

Encryption protects data in transit or storage; it does not decide whether the person you invited should see the document. See encryption basics for that separate topic.

Why can a classmate still see the document?

Check whether access comes through a shared folder, a group membership or another sharing link. Removing one individual invitation may leave another route to the file.
