Phishing, Spear Phishing and Email Spoofing

Phishing is a fake message that tries to make you click a link, open a file or type your password. This page explains the technical side: how attackers fake the sender of an email, how they build fake websites, and how email servers can detect them.

Phishing and spear phishing

  • Phishing: the same generic message is sent to thousands of people, for example "Your package is blocked, click here".
  • Spear phishing: a message written for one specific person or company. The attacker first collects information (name, job, boss, colleagues) from social networks and websites, so the email looks real.

How email spoofing works

The protocol used to send emails, SMTP, is very old and does not check who the sender really is. The "From" address that you see in your mail app is just text written by the sender, so an attacker can write any address there. 

From: "IT Help Desk" <helpdesk@university.example>
Subject: Your mailbox will be closed today

Attackers also use lookalike domains, which are very similar to real ones:

  • paypa1.com instead of paypal.com (number 1 instead of letter l)
  • microsoft-login-support.com (a real name inside a fake domain)
  • apple.com.account-check.net (the real domain is account-check.net)

Phishing infrastructure

To run a phishing campaign, attackers usually need:

  1. a fake domain, often with a free HTTPS certificate (so the padlock is shown);
  2. a server or hacked account to send many emails;
  3. a copy of a real login page that saves what the victim types;
  4. a way to receive the stolen passwords.

Today criminals can buy ready-made phishing kits that contain all these parts. Some kits even copy the session after login, so they can bypass codes sent by SMS or apps.

The defence: SPF, DKIM and DMARC

These three standards are saved in the DNS of a domain. They let the receiving server check if an email really comes from that domain.

Email authentication standards
Standard What it does
SPF Lists the servers that are allowed to send emails for the domain.
DKIM Adds a digital signature, so the receiver can check the email was not changed.
DMARC Says what to do if SPF or DKIM fail: nothing, move to spam, or reject.

Example of an SPF record:

v=spf1 ip4:192.0.2.10 -all

This means: only the server 192.0.2.10 can send emails for this domain, reject all the others. Since 2024 Gmail and Yahoo require these standards for anyone who sends many emails. They do not stop lookalike domains, because a fake domain can have its own SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

How to protect yourself

  • Check the real email address of the sender, not only the name.
  • Move the mouse over a link and read the domain before clicking.
  • Be careful with urgent messages ("today", "immediately").
  • Never send passwords or codes by email.
  • If you are not sure, open the website by typing the address yourself.
